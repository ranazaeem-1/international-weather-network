## Thumbnail creative direction — updated 17 October 2026

Read `iwn-brand-kit/thumbnail-workflow.md` before generating thumbnails. This is the current creative standard, superseding older moody/editorial thumbnail directions in previous prompts or brand-decision files; the three approved packaging designs above remain unchanged.

- Bright, detailed, immediately readable scene exposure is the default, including night scenes. Avoid crushed shadows, dull gray haze and dark cinematic grading. Fire glow alone is not adequate subject lighting.
- Prefer slightly elevated oblique aerial viewpoints that show affected streets, buildings and the extent of an event, with one large recognizable focal subject. Avoid routinely defaulting to eye-level rescue/truck scenes or very high aerial views with tiny subjects.
- Use short place-plus-action/consequence headlines with an event-supported curiosity hook. User-reported outliers: CALIFORNIA BURNING and PANAMA HIT AGAIN. These are creative references, not proof of CTR or causality. Avoid generic interchangeable text when a location and strong verb fit.
- Pair the headline with a visual that creates one clear unanswered question which the actual video answers. Do not substitute invented destruction, casualties, recurrence or locations for suspense.
- Check full-size, 320x180 and 160x90 readability before delivery. If the location, focal subject or consequence disappears at small size, revise the scene rather than adding more labels.
