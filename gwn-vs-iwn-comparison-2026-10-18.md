## 5. On-screen graphics
- **GWN:** a blue "BREAKING NEWS" lower-third with one fact per card ("16,000 ordered to evacuate", "2 firefighters hospitalized", "FEMA grant covers 75%…"). It appears about every 1–2 min. There's no scrolling ticker, and GWN watermarks are tiled down the blurred side bars. Most frames are clean, full-frame fire.
- **IWN (new format):** headline ticker + CTA ticker + lower-third twice per chapter + subscribe pill twice per chapter. In the Norte contact sheet a ticker or lower-third is on screen in about half the sampled frames.
- **Visual intensity:** GWN's sheet is mostly close flames, night fire lines, houses burning from the air, and aircraft. IWN's is more distant daytime smoke plumes and repeated backyard and street angles.

## 6. Titles
- **GWN formula:** `PLACE HIT by EVENT — BIG NUMBER/CONSEQUENCE (Latest Updates)`. It uses specific numbers in almost every title: 16,000, 7,200+, 46X IN 3 HOURS, 207,000, 120,000+, $2 BILLION, 2,700+.
- **GWN uses the best-known place name, even for a nearby fire.** "Malibu HIT by WILDFIRE — 16,000 Ordered to EVACUATE" is actually about the Norte Fire in San Clemente, and the description says so. It's an effective search tactic but technically inaccurate, so IWN shouldn't copy it literally. "SOUTHERN CALIFORNIA" or "ORANGE COUNTY" plus the number does the same job honestly.
- **IWN titles are vaguer** ("RAGES OUT OF CONTROL", "Flames Intensify, Homes Under Threat"). The best-performing IWN title is the one with the clearest hook ("HIT BY 3 WILDFIRES").

## 7. Thumbnails
- Both use near-identical packaging: AI-style scene, BREAKING NEWS tab, white headline box, logo top-left.
- GWN headlines: famous place + emotion + "!" ("CHAOS IN MALIBU!", "LA IN CHAOS!", "MALIBU IN DANGER!", "TURKEY FLOODED!").
- IWN: "CALIFORNIA BURNING", "MALIBU DESTROYED", "LA IN DANGER", "TURKEY SWEPT AWAY". **"MALIBU DESTROYED" overclaims**: no homes were lost in the Bryson Fire. That's a policy and credibility risk.

## 9. What to change (priority order)
1. **Publish faster and more often.** Aim for 6–12 h after the event, then post a second angle (a new number or new place) 8–12 h later from the same footage library. This is the largest measurable gap.
2. **Shorten the cold open to about 20 s** using the single strongest clip, and **keep audible eyewitness reactions**. Still mute reporters, profanity and music.
3. **Let strong clips run longer:** 10–15 s for the best shots, and keep fast cutting for filler. Retire the hard 8-second rule (already skipped for São Paulo).
4. **More narration, with context:** about 1,300–1,600 words and 55–60% of runtime. Open by anchoring the footage ("The video you just saw…") plus one open loop that the video answers later. Add 2–3 context chapters: history, what's next, the science.
5. **Thin the overlays.** GWN outperforms with one fact card every 1–2 min and no ticker. Consider an A/B test: the current dense overlays vs fact-card lower-thirds every ~90 s, the pill twice per video, and the ticker in about 3 chapters. *(The current density was your explicit request on 17 Oct. This is a data-driven suggestion to test, not a reversal.)*
6. **Titles:** a big number plus a well-known place plus the consequence, always true to the facts.
7. **Descriptions:**
   - Add an "In this video, you'll see…" paragraph and a SOURCES list.
   - Always include chapters and 15–20 tags.
   - Fix the hashtag and channel name in the Camp Pendleton upload, and add its chapters.
8. **Footage:** favour close flames, night fire, homes burning, and aircraft over distant smoke. Spend the search time there first.

*Caveat:* views depend heavily on channel size, upload time and YouTube's recommendation system, so these are correlations, not proof of cause. The strongest comparable signal is the per-subscriber and like-rate data, where IWN already leads.
