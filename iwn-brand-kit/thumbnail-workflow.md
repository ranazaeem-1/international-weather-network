# IWN thumbnail workflow

Updated 17 October 2026. Applies to new thumbnails and requested revisions. Packaging references are in `approved-thumbnail-designs/`; design selection remains governed by root `AGENTS.md`.

## 1. Establish the story and design

Read the episode brief and available verified facts. Confirm place, event, consequence and reporting cutoff; a draft claim is not independently verified merely because it appears in the folder. Ask which design (1, 2 or 3) only if the user has not selected one. Revisions to a supplied thumbnail inherit its selected design.

Keep approved logos, colors, borders, badge wording and headline-panel geometry. This update changes scene direction and headline selection, not the approved templates. Design 3 uses a Spanish main headline; LIVE and BREAKING NEWS stay in English.

## 2. Choose the hook before generating

Draft three short headline candidates internally, normally 2–4 words. Prefer a recognizable place plus a vivid action or consequence. Choose the strongest truthful pair of headline and visual; do not require another approval unless information is missing.

| Pattern | Example | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Place + ongoing event | CALIFORNIA BURNING | Fire is the actual story |
| Place + recurrence | PANAMA HIT AGAIN | A distinct repeat event is established |
| Place + consequence | TEXAS UNDERWATER | The video covers serious Texas flooding; image scope must match |
| Place + immediate stakes | MALIBU EVACUATED | Evacuation is supported; distinguish affected areas from whole-city claims |

CALIFORNIA BURNING and PANAMA HIT AGAIN are user-reported outliers. Treat them as promising references, not a proven causal rule. They are action-led headlines, rather than literal calls to action such as Subscribe.

Create curiosity through a specific tension: how close is fire to these homes, how far has this flood spread, what happened after another shock? The video must answer it. Avoid generic labels such as FLOOD CHAOS or DRIVERS TRAPPED when place-plus-action communicates more. Do not invent destruction, casualties or repeat events to strengthen copy. If the user supplies exact wording, preserve it and flag material conflicts with the episode brief.

## 3. Frame the scale of the event

Default to a slightly elevated, oblique aerial viewpoint: a low drone, rooftop or hillside overlook, not a near-vertical map view. Show a large recognizable subject plus enough surroundings to explain the extent and direction of danger.

- Flood: a neighborhood or road network with water across multiple streets and a clear foreground house or vehicle for scale.
- Wildfire: homes or infrastructure with a legible fire front and terrain connecting them; leave separation readable.
- Snow: a pass or neighborhood with an understandable blocked route and accumulation.
- Earthquake: an affected block with one clear damage focal point, only where supported.

Keep one dominant focal subject, one main hazard and supporting context. Avoid the repeated eye-level truck-and-rescue-boat formula. Eye-level is an exception when it tells the story better. Do not use a panorama so wide that everything becomes miniature. Reserve the logo and headline zones before composing.

## 4. Brightness and detail are mandatory

Use bright, clean editorial exposure, readable midtones and open shadows. Houses, streets, vehicles, water texture and terrain must remain distinguishable at thumbnail size. Night scenes retain a night sky while foreground detail is visibly illuminated by plausible streetlights, emergency lights or reflected light. Preserve bright flame and light detail instead of clipping it.

Do not default prompts to dark, moody, blue hour, charcoal smoke or cinematic gloom. Specify visible subject detail rather than relying on orange fire against black silhouettes. Avoid gray/brown veils, excessive smoke obscuring the scene, oversaturated orange casts and crunchy sharpening. Bright does not mean washed out; retain controlled contrast and distinct natural colors.

Drama should come from viewpoint, scale, proximity, motion and a clear visual hierarchy. AI artwork is illustrative, not documentary evidence; do not reconstruct unverified damage as a factual event photo.

## 5. Generate and review

Use the selected approved template as the layout reference. Include exact headline, camera height/angle, primary subject, hazard, lighting, detail requirements and elements to preserve in the prompt. Request clarity at small viewing sizes, not merely more pixels.

For requested alternatives, vary the actual concept: neighborhood scale, property threat, or transport disruption. Different crops or colors of the same scene do not count as distinct concepts. Keep the requested number of outputs; do not generate unnecessary variants.

Review at full size, 320x180 and 160x90:

- Can the headline be read immediately, with no cramped letters or clipped accents?
- Are the main subject and hazard identifiable without zooming?
- Does the elevated view explain broader impact without losing the focal point?
- Are important shadows and night details still readable?
- Does the visual work with the headline to raise one specific question?
- Are location, event and consequence consistent with the actual video?
- Are logo, typography, framing and badge colors faithful to the chosen design?

Revise failed criteria before export. Compare with the supplied references when accessible; never claim to have inspected a competitor gallery that failed to load.

## 6. Delivery and learning

### User clarification — 19 October 2026

Follow GWN's visual approach for the base scene: layered foreground, middle distance and background; broader views of affected areas; recognizable scale cues; distinct natural colors; and balanced exposure that keeps subjects readable under storm clouds. More useful visible detail matters more than simply adding foam, smoke or sharpening. Preserve coherent lighting and believable scene geometry. Use original scenes and event-supported consequences.

The user explicitly retained the existing headline strip and LIVE badge. Keep their established sizes, placement and typography; do not shrink them to match GWN. Preserve the selected approved design's logo, borders and colors as well. This supersedes the assistant's suggestion to test more compact packaging. Apply GWN-inspired changes to the base visual within the approved packaging.

Preserve originals and version revisions. Save PNG master, 1280x720 JPG and generation prompts in the project; describe AI illustrations accurately. A larger export alone is not added native detail.

When analytics are supplied, record impressions, CTR, traffic source, observation window and retention/watch time alongside headline and visual direction. Public views or two outliers alone cannot isolate thumbnail performance from topic, timing and distribution. Test one major variable at a time where practical, then update this workflow from evidence.
